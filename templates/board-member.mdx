A member of a public housing authority board holds governing authority over the entire organization. The board is collectively responsible for policy, oversight, budgeting, and accountability of the executive director and all operations below. Individual board members are accountable to the appointing authority and, through that chain, ultimately to the public and the State of Illinois.

## What the Board Is Collectively Responsible For

<Note>
  - Appointing, evaluating, and if necessary removing the Executive Director
  - Adopting and maintaining policies governing all WCHA operations
  - Approving and monitoring the annual budget
  - Ensuring compliance with HUD requirements, state housing law, and WCHA bylaws
  - Reviewing and acting on reports of problems, complaints, or deficiencies
  - Conducting or commissioning audits
  - Making meetings, minutes, and records available to the public
</Note>

## What Cannot Be Delegated Away

<Warning>
  A board cannot escape accountability by delegating all decisions to an executive director. The board's duty is to oversee, not merely to receive reports. If the board approved a budget that eliminated safety inspections, adopted a policy that created a compliance failure, or failed to act on reported problems, that is a board-level accountability question — not only an executive-director question.
</Warning>

## Questions to Ask This Board Member

<Accordion title="About oversight">
  How often does the board receive reports from the ED? What topics are covered? Are written reports provided?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="About policy">
  What policies has the board adopted for maintenance, safety, staffing, and complaints? When were they last reviewed?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="About the ED">
  How is the ED evaluated? Has the board ever taken corrective action against an ED?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="About finances">
  Who audits WCHA? Are audit reports public? Has the board received any findings of concern?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="About HUD">
  Has WCHA received any HUD notices of deficiency, corrective action, or compliance violations? What did the board do in response?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="About this member">

</Accordion>

## Escalation

If this board member fails to respond to public inquiries or if records show systemic problems, escalation options include:

- **Appointing authority**: The person or body that appointed this commissioner can be asked why they have not acted on reported problems.
- **Wayne County Board / Executive**: County officials can be asked what oversight they exercise over WCHA and its board.
- **Illinois Housing Development Authority (IHDA)**: IHDA oversees public housing authorities in Illinois and can receive complaints about board conduct or authority management.
- **HUD**: The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development can investigate complaints about housing authority compliance and board accountability.
- **Illinois Attorney General**: The Public Access Counselor can assist with FOIA disputes and open meetings compliance.
- **Local media and residents**: Public accountability often depends on residents and journalists raising questions at board meetings and in public forums.
