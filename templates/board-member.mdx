A board commissioner of the Wayne County Housing Authority holds governing authority over a public body funded with public money. This is not an honorary position. The commissioner votes on budgets, approves or removes the executive director, sets policy, and is legally responsible for the authority's conduct. This profile documents that public authority, and traces the commissioner's accountability upward and downward.

## Accountability Chain

<Steps>
  <Step title="This Commissioner">
    Holds governing authority. Votes on budgets, hires and fires the executive director, approves policy. Present or absent at meetings. Votes or abstains. Has access to audit reports, financial records, and compliance status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Full Board">
    The Board acts collectively. Individual commissioners contribute to or obstruct effective governance. A commissioner who consistently misses meetings, votes without reading materials, or ignores reported problems is not meeting their duty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wayne County">
    The commissioner was appointed by someone in county government. That appointing authority is accountable for who they put on this board and for removing commissioners who fail in their duties.
  </Step>
  <Step title="State of Illinois">
    Board commissioners of public housing authorities operate under Illinois law. State oversight applies to the authority as a whole, including the board's governance conduct.
  </Step>
  <Step title="HUD">
    Federal housing program governance requirements apply to board conduct. HUD can sanction authorities with boards that fail to provide adequate oversight.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The Governing Duty

A board commissioner's primary duty is not to be friendly with the executive director or rubber-stamp the budget. The duty is genuine oversight: reading financial reports, asking hard questions, acting on audit findings, and ensuring the authority serves its public mission. Commissioners who do not exercise this duty remain legally and publicly accountable for the results.

<Note>
  The board cannot fully delegate its oversight responsibility to the executive director. The executive director reports to the board. The board does not report to the executive director. An executive director who hides problems from the board does not excuse the board from accountability if the board had access to records that should have triggered inquiry.
</Note>

## Questions for This Commissioner

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Attendance">
    What is the attendance record? Are missed meetings documented? How many meetings were held in the past 12 months, and how many did this commissioner attend? Is there a pattern of absence around votes on sensitive matters?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Budget">
    Did the commissioner review the budget before voting? Were questions asked about maintenance and staffing? Did the commissioner request line-item detail or accept the budget as presented? Were reserves discussed?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Complaints">
    Was the commissioner aware of resident or employee complaints? What action was taken? Were complaints reported to the full board? Was follow-up documented? Did the commissioner request complaint logs?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Audit">
    Was the commissioner present when audit results were presented? What was the response? Were findings acted upon? Was a corrective action plan approved? Was the auditor independent?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Executive Director">
    What evaluation has been conducted? What are the performance standards? Is the evaluation documented? Has the board ever declined to renew or removed an executive director? On what basis?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
