The Wayne County Housing Authority is a public body created under Illinois law to provide affordable housing in Wayne County. Its decisions directly affect residents. Its funding comes from public sources. Its board holds public authority. This page documents not just the authority's structure. It shows how every decision, policy, contract, and failure connects upward to county government, Illinois state oversight, and federal housing accountability.

## Accountability Chain

<Steps>
  <Step title="Housing Authority Staff">
    Frontline employees carry out the authority's work. Their performance, training, supervision, and working conditions determine what actually happens on the ground. Staff problems that are not addressed by management become management failures.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Executive Director">
    Appointed by the Board, the Executive Director is responsible for day-to-day operations, staff supervision, compliance, contracting, and financial management. The Executive Director's performance reflects on the Board that hired and retained them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Board of Commissioners">
    The Board is the governing authority. It appoints the Executive Director, approves budgets, sets policy, receives financial reports, and is legally responsible for the authority's compliance with state and federal requirements. A board that fails to exercise genuine oversight bears accountability for the consequences.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wayne County">
    The authority was created under Illinois law and operates in Wayne County. The county may appoint board members and maintain oversight responsibilities. County government cannot disclaim awareness of the authority's conduct if that conduct was documented in public records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="State of Illinois">
    The Illinois Housing Development Authority (IHDA), the Illinois Comptroller, and other state agencies have audit, licensing, and compliance authority over public housing authorities. Illinois statutes define how authorities are created, governed, and held accountable. The state is always part of the chain.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Federal">
    HUD funds and oversees public housing programs. Authorities that receive HUD funding are subject to federal compliance requirements, audits, and enforcement.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Questions at Each Level

## Records to Request

| Record | Why It Matters | Where to Request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board meeting minutes and agendas | Shows what the board actually reviews and approves | Housing Authority or Wayne County Clerk |
| Financial audits and compliance reports | Reveals financial health and regulatory compliance | IHDA, HUD, or Illinois Comptroller |
| Executive Director contract and performance evaluations | Documents board oversight of executive leadership | Housing Authority (FOIA) |
| Staffing records and training logs | Shows whether staff have resources and qualifications | Housing Authority (FOIA) |
| Complaint records and resolutions | Reveals whether problems are documented and addressed | Housing Authority (FOIA) |
| HUD inspection reports and corrective action plans | Documents federal oversight and any deficiencies | HUD Office of Public Housing or FOIA |

## Escalation Paths

| If This Happens | Escalate To | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board fails to respond to records request | Wayne County State's Attorney | FOIA enforcement referral |
| Suspected financial mismanagement | Illinois Comptroller | Audit request or hotline |
| HUD compliance failure | HUD Office of Public Housing | Complaint or records request |
| Unsafe housing conditions | Illinois Attorney General, HUD | Consumer protection or housing complaint |
| Retaliation for reporting problems | Illinois Department of Labor, HUD | Whistleblower or fair housing complaint |
