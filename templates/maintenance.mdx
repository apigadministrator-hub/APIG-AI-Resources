## What This Worker May Know

The frontline maintenance worker has direct knowledge of ground conditions, equipment, supplies, work assignments, safety concerns, and day-to-day operations. This worker is often the first to know about hazards, needed repairs, missing equipment, or problems with supervision and training. Their observations are primary evidence.

## Questions to Ask at Each Level

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Worker">
    What were you assigned to do? Who gave that assignment? Were you trained? Was equipment provided? Did you report any unsafe conditions? Were you supervised?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Executive Director">
    Who directed the maintenance schedule? What policies govern cemetery maintenance? Is there a written maintenance plan? Are inspections conducted? What training is provided?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Board of Commissioners">
    Did the board adopt a maintenance policy? Did the board approve adequate staffing and equipment? Did the board receive any reports of maintenance problems? Are meeting minutes available?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Appointing Authority">
    Who appointed the board? Were appointments in compliance with applicable law or bylaws? Has the appointing authority received any complaints about WCHA operations?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wayne County">
    Does the county have an oversight role? Does the county fund WCHA? Has the county board received or reviewed any WCHA audits or complaints?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="State of Illinois">
    Is WCHA subject to IHDA oversight? Has Illinois conducted any inspections or audits of WCHA? Does state law impose maintenance standards on public housing authorities? Has federal HUD conducted any inspections?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What Cannot Be Delegated Away

<Warning>
  The fact that a maintenance worker physically performed a task does not transfer responsibility for training, policy, equipment, or supervision to that worker. The governing board cannot delegate accountability for operations it was required to oversee. The appointing authority cannot avoid accountability by pointing to the board it appointed.
</Warning>
