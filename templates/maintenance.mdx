## The Accountability Chain

<Steps>
  <Step title="This Employee">
    The groundskeeper performs the physical work. They are the source of direct observation, physical evidence, and frontline knowledge. They may report conditions up the chain — or fail to. Their employment is public, their pay is public, and their duties are documented in public records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Direct Supervisor / Executive Director">
    The Executive Director (or designated maintenance supervisor) is responsible for assigning, directing, training, evaluating, and disciplining this worker. If the work is inadequate, the question becomes: Was the supervisor aware? Did they inspect the work? Did they respond to complaints? Did they provide adequate resources, training, and time?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wayne County Housing Authority Board of Commissioners">
    The Board governs the authority. It approves budgets (including staffing and maintenance budgets), sets policy, hires the Executive Director, and is responsible for overall oversight. A Board that chronically underfunds maintenance, ignores complaints, or fails to evaluate the Executive Director bears accountability for the resulting conditions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wayne County">
    The County is the parent governmental jurisdiction. It may have appointed board members, provided funding, or maintained oversight relationships with the authority. County government cannot disclaim responsibility for the conduct of entities it created, funds, or appoints.
  </Step>
  <Step title="State of Illinois and Federal Oversight">
    The Wayne County Housing Authority is a public body created under Illinois law. It may receive HUD funding, making it subject to federal oversight. The Illinois Housing Development Authority, the Illinois Comptroller, and other state agencies may have audit, inspection, or compliance authority. Illinois statutes govern how public authorities are created, funded, and held accountable.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Cannot Be Delegated

<Note>
  A supervisor cannot avoid accountability by saying "I didn't know" when it was their job to know. A board cannot avoid accountability by saying "we relied on the director" when the director's failure was documented in records before the board. Delegation of duties does not transfer accountability for those duties.
</Note>

## Questions at Each Level

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="This Employee">
    What are your exact duties? What did you observe? What did you report? Were you trained? Did you have the tools and time to do the job? Were you ever told not to report something?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Supervisor / Executive Director">
    Were you aware of the conditions? When? What did you do? What performance evaluations exist? What inspection records exist? What complaints were received and how were they handled?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Housing Authority Board">
    What maintenance reports were presented to the board? What was the maintenance budget? Was an inspection or audit conducted? What did it find? Were there complaints before the board?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wayne County">
    Did the county receive complaints? Did it conduct oversight of the authority? Who appointed the board? On what basis were appointments made?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="State of Illinois / HUD">
    Is the authority in compliance with applicable state and federal requirements? When was the last state or federal inspection? What were the findings? Is the authority's grant status current?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
