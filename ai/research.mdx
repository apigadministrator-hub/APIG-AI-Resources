Research in APIG is a structured process. An AI agent does not browse the web and summarize results. It identifies a specific research question, consults applicable sources, records what each source says, attaches provenance, and flags uncertainty. Every finding is a proposed record with a source, not a confident assertion.

## What AI Researches

AI agents may research the following types of public sources:

- Government websites
- Official agency pages
- Meeting minutes
- Public records databases
- Election records
- Court records
- News archives
- FOIA responses
- Other authorized public sources

All sources must be documented with URLs and retrieval dates where available.

## How Findings Are Recorded

Each research output includes the following fields:

- The question asked
- Sources consulted (with URLs and retrieval dates)
- Findings per source
- Proposed record
- Verification status
- Confidence notes
- Relevant uncertainties

Do not collapse multiple sources into a single undifferentiated summary. Preserve what each source says separately.

## Source Hierarchy for Research

Prefer primary government sources over secondary news. When only secondary sources are available, record the secondary source and note that primary source confirmation is needed. A news article reporting on a government action is not equivalent to the government document itself.

## Conflicting Findings

When sources disagree, preserve both findings. Do not silently choose one. Flag the conflict and mark the proposed record as Conflicting. The conflict itself is a finding and should be reported to human reviewers for resolution.

## Unknown Remains Unknown

AI must not invent positions, people, organizations, or relationships to complete a record. If information cannot be established from available sources, it remains Unknown. A record with Unknown fields is preferable to a record with fabricated fields.

<Tip>
  A research report that identifies gaps and conflicts is more valuable than one that fills them with guesses.
</Tip>
