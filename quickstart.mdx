APIG is an investigative information system. This guide explains what it tracks, how information is structured, and how to find what you are looking for.

## What APIG Tracks

APIG collects and connects information about:

- **People** — individuals who hold or have held public positions
- **Positions** — specific roles or titles within organizations
- **Organizations** — government agencies, departments, offices, and other entities
- **Jurisdictions** — geographic areas such as states, counties, cities, and districts
- **Documents** — public records, filings, reports, and other source materials
- **Meetings** — scheduled or recorded gatherings of public bodies
- **Sources** — citations and references that support each claim
- **Relationships** — documented connections between people, positions, organizations, and documents

## How Information Is Organized

APIG uses an entity model that keeps each type of information separate and clearly linked:

- Every **person** is separate from their **position**. A person can hold multiple positions over time, and each position can be held by different people over time.
- Every **position** is separate from its **organization**. An organization can have many positions, and positions can move between organizations when documented.
- **Relationships** are documented with **sources**. Any connection between entities includes a reference to where that information came from and a verification status.

This structure makes it possible to track changes over time and to see exactly where each piece of information originated.

## Navigating APIG

<Steps>
  <Step title="Browse by jurisdiction">
    Start from a state or county and navigate down through agencies to offices to people. This top-down approach helps you understand the structure of government in a specific area.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search by name">
    Search for a person, agency, or organization directly. The search looks across all entity types, so you can jump straight to the record you need.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow relationships">
    From any entity, follow documented relationships to connected people, organizations, and documents. This lets you move sideways through the data and discover connections you might not have expected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review sources">
    Every claim links back to a source document or record. Check the source to understand the basis for the information and to verify it yourself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Verification and Sources

APIG shows where information came from. Each claim has a verification status that indicates whether it has been confirmed, is pending review, or is currently unknown. Unknown is a valid value: it means the system does not yet have enough information to confirm or deny the claim. Always review the linked source before relying on any piece of information for critical decisions.
