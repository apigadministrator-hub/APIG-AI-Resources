The Accountability Project, or APIG, is a public-interest investigative information system built to research, organize, document, and preserve publicly relevant information about governments, agencies, organizations, people, documents, meetings, and public records. We are not a traditional news outlet. We are a civic infrastructure project: a structured, searchable, and permanent record of who holds power, what they do, and how it is documented.

## What We Do

APIG exists to make public information actually public. Government produces an enormous volume of records, minutes, filings, and decisions. Much of it is scattered across websites, buried in PDFs, locked behind FOIA processes, or simply lost to time. We collect that material, verify it, organize it geographically, and keep it accessible.

Our work is evidence-based. Every claim in our system traces to a source. When a fact cannot be established, we mark it unknown. We do not invent facts, infer motives, or fill gaps with speculation. Unknown is a valid and honest value.

## Where We Started

APIG began in Illinois, with a focus on Wayne County. The site is organized geographically so users can navigate from the state level down through counties, cities and townships, agencies, and individual people. This geographic drill-down is the core of how we present jurisdiction and accountability.

## Historical Preservation

Records in the APIG system are preserved, not deleted when circumstances change. An official who leaves office, an agency that is reorganized, or a document that is later amended all remain in the system as historical records. The record of what was true at a given time is itself a public fact worth preserving.

## Two Identities, One Purpose

The Accountability Project (APIG) and A Pig Monster are distinct and separate entities.

- **APIG** is the investigative information system: structured data, public records, verified facts, and historical preservation.
- **A Pig Monster** is the news media arm: original reporting, analysis, and narrative journalism.

They share a commitment to transparency and evidence, but they operate independently. APIG does not publish news stories. A Pig Monster does not maintain the database. The separation keeps both functions clear and accountable.
