Every page on the Accountability Project follows one rule: a worker who mows a cemetery for a public housing authority is the starting point of inquiry, not the endpoint. The question is not only what that worker did. The question is who directed them, who trained them, who set the policy, who funds the operation, who governs the authority, and whether the State of Illinois has any oversight, licensing, funding, or regulatory role. Every individual action or condition connects upward through a complete chain of authority.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The Frontline Worker">
    The person who physically performed the act, has direct knowledge of the condition, or is the source of direct evidence. This is where every inquiry begins, not where it ends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Immediate Supervisor">
    The person who directed, assigned, or had day-to-day authority over the worker. Supervisors are responsible for knowing what their workers do and correcting unsafe or improper practices.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Department or Office">
    The entity responsible for staffing, training, policy, and operational standards. A department without written policies or training programs has failed its basic administrative duty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Appointing or Hiring Authority">
    The person or body that placed the department leadership in their role. This authority is responsible for selecting competent leadership and holding that leadership accountable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Governing Board or Commission">
    The body with oversight authority, budget approval, and policy-setting power for the entity. The board cannot claim ignorance of conditions it is statutorily required to oversee.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The Municipal, County, or Township Administration">
    The government unit that funds, controls, or is legally responsible for the entity. This level holds the power of appointment, removal, and budget allocation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The State of Illinois">
    The state agency, statute, regulation, funding stream, licensing requirement, or administrative rule that applies. State law often creates the legal framework that local entities must follow.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Responsibility for policy, training, budgeting, oversight, and compliance cannot be fully delegated to a frontline worker. If a worker lacks training, that reflects on the supervisor. If a department lacks a policy, that reflects on the governing board. If a governing board lacks oversight capacity, that reflects on the appointing authority. Delegation of a task does not transfer the legal or administrative responsibility of the superior authority.
</Warning>

## What to Look For at Each Level

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Frontline Worker">
    What did they do, say, or observe? What records exist (timesheets, work orders, incident reports)? Were they trained? Were they supervised? Did they report the condition up the chain? Do they have written instructions or standard operating procedures?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Immediate Supervisor">
    Did they know about the condition or action? Did they direct it? Did they report it? What logs, notes, or communications exist? Did they conduct inspections or site visits? Did they document corrective actions?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Department or Office">
    Does a written policy exist? Is there a training program? Are inspections conducted? Are maintenance records kept? Is there a complaint or incident log? Are contracts and vendor agreements on file?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Appointing or Hiring Authority">
    What criteria were used to select department leadership? Are there performance evaluations? Did this authority receive reports of problems? What power does this authority have to remove or discipline leadership?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Governing Board">
    Did the board adopt a policy? Did the board receive reports? Did the board approve the budget for staffing, training, and equipment? Are meeting minutes public? Did the board vote on contracts or significant operational changes?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Municipal or County Administration">
    Is the entity funded by the county or municipality? Who appoints the board? Who can remove the executive director? What audit or oversight process exists? Is there an intergovernmental agreement?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="State of Illinois">
    Does Illinois law regulate this type of entity? Is the entity licensed, funded, or audited by a state agency? Does Illinois HUD, DCEO, or another agency have jurisdiction? What state statutes apply? Are there federal funding conditions that the state administers?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What Records Establish Responsibility

| Level | Records to Request |
| --- | --- |
| Frontline Worker | Timesheets, work orders, attendance, incident reports, disciplinary records |
| Supervisor | Supervisory logs, communications, performance reviews, complaint responses |
| Department | Policies, training records, inspection logs, maintenance schedules, contracts |
| Governing Board | Meeting minutes, agendas, budgets, resolutions, audit reports, correspondence |
| Municipal/County | Appointment records, contracts, funding agreements, audit reports, FOIA logs |
| State of Illinois | Licensing records, audit findings, inspection reports, funding conditions, regulatory correspondence |

## Remedies and Escalation

| Level | Available Action |
| --- | --- |
| Internal | Complaint to supervisor, department head, or board |
| Administrative | FOIA request, audit request, open meetings complaint |
| State | Complaint to Illinois AG, IHDA, DCEO, or applicable state agency |
| Federal | HUD complaint (for housing authorities), federal FOIA, federal inspector general |
| Legal | Civil suit, injunction, mandamus |
| Political | Elected official contact, public comment at board meeting, press |
