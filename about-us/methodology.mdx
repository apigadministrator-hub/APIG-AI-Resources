Every entry in the Accountability Project is the result of a deliberate process: identifying a public fact, locating its source, recording the evidence, and assigning a verification status. This page explains how that process works and what standards govern it.

## Primary Sources

We rely on primary sources wherever possible. These include:

- Public records and official documents
- Government websites and published filings
- FOIA request responses
- Meeting minutes and agendas
- Court records and dockets
- Campaign finance and ethics disclosures

Secondary sources, including news coverage, are used for context and leads, but they do not replace direct documentation.

## Verification Status

Every record in the APIG system carries a verification status. The possible statuses are:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Verified | The fact is supported by direct evidence from a primary source. |
| Unverified | The fact has been reported or suggested but lacks direct confirmation. |
| Disputed | Credible sources conflict, or the fact is actively contested. |
| Historical | The fact was true at a documented time but may no longer be current. |
| Unknown | The fact could not be established; no reliable source was found. |

Unknown is a valid and intentional status. If a fact cannot be established, we mark it unknown rather than guess. We do not fill gaps with inference or assumption.

## Source Attribution

Every significant claim traces to a specific source. Where the source is a public document, we link to it or preserve it. Where the source is a FOIA response, we note the request and response date. Where the source is a government website, we record the URL and the date of access. Transparency about where information comes from is as important as the information itself.

## No Guilt by Association

We document relationships, affiliations, and organizational proximity because those facts are relevant to understanding power and influence. We do not imply wrongdoing based on association alone. Serving on a board, attending a meeting, or being employed by an agency is a fact, not an accusation.

## Legal Characterization Preserved

We preserve the legal character of claims exactly as they appear in source material:

- Allegations remain allegations.
- Charges remain charges.
- Convictions remain convictions.

We do not upgrade or downgrade legal language. An allegation is not a conviction. A dismissed charge is not an acquittal. The distinction matters, and we preserve it.

## Human Review

Research and organization are assisted by automated tools, but substantive publication in the APIG system requires human review. A person evaluates the source, checks the characterization, assigns the verification status, and confirms that the record meets our standards before it appears in the system.
