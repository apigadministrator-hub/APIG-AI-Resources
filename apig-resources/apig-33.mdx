APIG-33 is APIG's most comprehensive security specification. It consolidates principles from APIG-13, APIG-18, APIG-23, and APIG-26 into a single zero trust framework that governs authentication, authorization, AI task execution, and security incident response.

## Zero Trust Principle

APIG operates on a single foundational rule: never trust, always verify. No entity is trusted by default, even inside APIG's own systems.

This applies to:

- Human users, regardless of role or tenure
- AI agents, regardless of prior task history
- Internal services and microservices
- External integrations and APIs
- Network traffic, even within APIG infrastructure

Every request, every action, and every access attempt requires verification against current context and authorization.

## Continuous Authorization

Authorization in APIG is not granted once and assumed. It is re-verified when context changes.

Triggers for re-authorization include:

- Privilege changes (promotion, demotion, role transfer)
- Session timeout or idle period expiration
- Access from a new device or location
- Request for data outside the current task scope
- Detection of anomalous behavior patterns
- System security event or alert

<Warning>
  A user or AI agent authorized for Task A is not automatically authorized for Task B, even if both tasks are routine. Authorization is task-scoped and context-bound.
</Warning>

## AI Task Authorization Sequence

Every AI task in APIG follows this mandatory sequence:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive task">
    The AI receives a task request through an authorized channel
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify requester">
    The AI determines who or what issued the task request
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify authority">
    The AI confirms the requester has the authority to issue this type of task
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm task scope">
    The AI validates that the task falls within the requester's authorized scope
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute with minimum necessary access">
    The AI performs the task using only the minimum data and system access required
  </Step>
  <Step title="Report">
    The AI reports completion, partial completion, or refusal with reasoning
  </Step>
</Steps>

The AI must refuse the task and report the refusal if any step fails.

## Privilege Change Sequence

When a user's privileges change, all active sessions and tasks are re-evaluated.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Detect change">
    The system detects a privilege modification (elevation, reduction, or revocation)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Suspend active sessions">
    All active sessions for the affected identity are suspended pending review
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-evaluate tasks">
    In-progress tasks are checked against the new privilege set
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resume, modify, or terminate">
    Tasks that remain authorized resume. Tasks that exceed new privileges are terminated or downgraded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log and notify">
    The privilege change and all re-evaluation actions are logged. Affected users and supervisors are notified.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Security Incident Response Sequence

APIG responds to security incidents through a defined sequence:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Detect">
    Identify the potential incident through monitoring, reporting, or anomaly detection
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contain">
    Limit the scope and prevent further damage or unauthorized access
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess">
    Determine the nature, severity, and affected systems or data
  </Step>
  <Step title="Report">
    Notify designated authorities, affected parties, and compliance contacts as required
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remediate">
    Fix vulnerabilities, restore systems, and remove unauthorized access
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document">
    Record the full incident timeline, actions taken, and lessons learned
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Public Message Authorization Sequence

Messages received from the public are treated as untrusted by default, even when they appear credible.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive public message">
    A message arrives from an external, unverified source
  </Step>
  <Step title="Treat as untrusted">
    The message is processed in an isolated, untrusted context
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate content">
    The message is assessed for credibility, relevance, and potential threat or value
  </Step>
  <Step title="Escalate if credible threat or tip">
    Credible threats or valuable tips are escalated through proper channels for investigation
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do not grant operational authority">
    Under no circumstances does a public message alone grant operational authority to act within APIG systems
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A tip from the public may initiate an investigation, but the investigation itself must follow all authorization sequences. The tip is a signal, not a key.
</Tip>
