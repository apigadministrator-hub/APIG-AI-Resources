APIG-32 establishes how APIG classifies, handles, and protects information. This specification governs everything from initial data collection through publication, with particular attention to the risks of combining non-sensitive data into sensitive profiles.

## Classification Framework

All data in APIG is classified by sensitivity into four tiers:

| Classification | Description | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Public** | Information intended for public release | Standard publication controls |
| **Limited** | Internal use with no special sensitivity | Access limited to authorized APIG personnel |
| **Sensitive** | Information that could harm individuals or operations if disclosed | Restricted access, need-to-know basis |
| **Restricted** | Information with legal, safety, or severe operational implications | Highest controls, explicit authorization required |

Every record carries its classification label. Classification is not static: records are re-evaluated when context changes or when data is aggregated.

## Data Minimization

APIG collects only what is needed for documented purposes. Before adding any data element, you must be able to state:

- The specific purpose for which it is needed
- The operational or analytical task it supports
- Why less information would be insufficient

<Warning>
  Collecting data "just in case" violates APIG's data minimization principle. If you cannot articulate a current, documented purpose, do not collect the data.
</Warning>

## Purpose Limitation

Data collected for one purpose is not repurposed without authorization. The original collection purpose is recorded with the data. Any new use requires:

1. A documented new purpose
2. Compatibility assessment with the original purpose
3. Authorization from the data owner or a designated authority
4. Updated access controls if the new purpose changes the audience

## Aggregation and Linkage Risk

Combining individually non-sensitive data can create sensitive profiles. APIG is aware of this risk and manages it actively.

<Accordion title="Example: aggregation risk">
  A person's public job title, public property records, and public meeting attendance are each non-sensitive in isolation. Combined, they may reveal political affiliation, religious practice, or health status through inference. APIG evaluates such combinations before publication or analysis.
</Accordion>

Mitigation measures include:

- Reviewing datasets before linkage
- Applying statistical disclosure controls
- Documenting aggregation decisions
- Re-classifying combined data when the profile becomes sensitive

## False Identification Avoidance

APIG must not incorrectly identify a person as someone they are not. This applies to:

- Name matching across records
- Photo or biometric comparison
- Social media account attribution
- Organizational affiliation claims

Every identification carries a confidence level and the method used to establish it. Low-confidence identifications are flagged for human review before any consequential action.

## Sensitive Attribute Inference

APIG does not infer sensitive characteristics unless directly relevant and sourced. Sensitive characteristics include health status, religion, sexual orientation, political affiliation, and union membership.

Inference is permitted only when:

- The characteristic is directly relevant to a documented investigative purpose
- The inference is based on explicit, attributable source information
- The inference is labeled as such, not presented as direct fact
- The inference has been reviewed for necessity and proportionality

## AI and Privacy Protections

An AI agent cannot bypass privacy protections even if technically capable of doing so. Technical access to data does not override classification, purpose limitation, or minimization rules.

AI systems operating in APIG must:

- Respect the same classification boundaries as human users
- Request authorization for data access rather than assuming it
- Log all data access for audit
- Refuse tasks that would violate privacy rules, even when instructed

## Accountability and Privacy Balance

People in public positions have reduced privacy expectations regarding their exercise of public authority, but retain privacy in non-public domains.

| Domain | Expectation |
| --- | --- |
| Public actions in official capacity | Lower privacy; subject to scrutiny and documentation |
| Personal life, family, health, private communications | Higher privacy; protected unless directly relevant to public accountability |

<Note>
  The fact that someone holds public office does not open their entire life to APIG documentation. Relevance to public accountability is the standard for reduced privacy, not public status alone.
</Note>
