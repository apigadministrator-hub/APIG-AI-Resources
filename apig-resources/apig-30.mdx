APIG-30 establishes how APIG evaluates claims, sources, and evidence before reaching conclusions. Every claim follows a structured hierarchy from assertion to confidence assessment. Corroboration must be independent. Circular reporting is identified and rejected. Misconduct claims require evidence before publication. Accountability claims distinguish direct conduct from supervisory, oversight, and appointment relationships. Legal characterizations are preserved at every stage.

## Claim, Source, Evidence, Verification, Confidence, Conclusion Hierarchy

Every claim APIG considers follows this path:

1. **Claim**: The assertion being evaluated
2. **Source**: The origin of the claim
3. **Evidence**: The material supporting the claim
4. **Verification**: The process of confirming the evidence
5. **Confidence**: The assessed reliability of the claim after verification
6. **Conclusion**: The documented outcome, qualified by confidence level

No stage is skipped. A claim without a source is untraceable. A source without evidence is unsubstantiated. Evidence without verification is unconfirmed. Verification without confidence assessment is incomplete.

## Corroboration Must Be Independent

Sources must have independent access to facts. Circular reporting does not corroborate.

<Warning>
  Multiple outlets reporting the same unverified original source creates a false appearance of corroboration. APIG treats these as a single source, not as independent confirmation.
</Warning>

Independent corroboration requires that sources:

- Obtained information through separate channels
- Did not rely on the same original report
- Can describe their own access to the underlying facts

## Circular Reporting Problem

The circular reporting problem occurs when:

1. Source A publishes an unverified claim
2. Source B repeats Source A's claim, adding no original reporting
3. Source C cites both A and B as evidence the claim is widely reported
4. The multiplicity of reports creates an illusion of independent confirmation

APIG identifies circular reporting by tracing claims to their earliest documented origin and evaluating whether subsequent sources added independent evidence.

## Misconduct Claims Need Evidence

APIG does not publish misconduct claims without evidence. The presence of an allegation is not sufficient. The allegation must be supported by evidence that meets APIG's evaluation standards.

Evidence types that may support misconduct claims include:

- Documentary records (emails, contracts, financial statements)
- Witness testimony with identifiable sources
- Official findings from recognized proceedings
- Physical or digital artifacts with verifiable provenance

## Accountability Claims Must Distinguish Relationships

Accountability claims must distinguish direct conduct from supervisory, oversight, and appointment relationships. A person who appointed a supervisor is not automatically responsible for the supervisor's day-to-day decisions. A board that provides oversight is not automatically responsible for operational errors.

The relationship types defined in APIG-28 govern how accountability is mapped. The evaluation process asks:

- What was the nature of the relationship at the time?
- What authority did the relationship confer?
- What actions fell within that authority?
- What evidence connects the person to the specific conduct in question?

## Evidence Evaluation Sequence

APIG applies a consistent sequence for every significant claim:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the claim">
    State the assertion clearly and specifically. Vague claims cannot be evaluated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify the source">
    Determine who originated the claim and what access they had to the underlying facts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate source quality">
    Assess the source's reliability, proximity to the facts, and potential bias or motive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify corroborating sources">
    Find additional sources that address the same claim with their own evidence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess independence">
    Verify that corroborating sources did not derive their information from the original source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine confidence level">
    Assign a confidence rating based on source quality, evidence strength, and corroboration independence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document conclusion">
    Record the conclusion, the confidence level, and the reasoning that supports it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Legal Characterization Is Preserved

APIG preserves legal characterizations at every stage of the evaluation process:

| Term | Meaning | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Allegation** | An unproven claim of wrongdoing | Used when a claim has been made but no formal process has evaluated it |
| **Charge** | A formal accusation by an authorized body | Used when a prosecutor or regulatory body has filed charges |
| **Conviction** | A finding of guilt by a court | Used only when a court has issued a final judgment |

APIG does not upgrade characterizations. An allegation remains an allegation until a charge is filed. A charge remains a charge until a conviction is entered. This preserves accuracy and avoids prejudgment.
