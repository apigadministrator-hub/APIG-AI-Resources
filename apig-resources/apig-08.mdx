APIG-08 defines how the AP Investigative Group models authority, accountability, and chain-of-command relationships within its knowledge graph. This specification establishes the authority graph structure and imposes a critical rule: accountability navigation does not propagate guilt or liability upward through the hierarchy.

## Authority Types

APIG tracks multiple types of authority relationships between entities. Each type is explicitly classified and stored as a labeled edge in the authority graph.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Supervisory" icon="sitemap">
    Direct managerial oversight of a subordinate position or person.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Appointment" icon="handshake">
    The power to appoint a person to a position or role.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Removal" icon="user-xmark">
    The power to remove a person from a position or role.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Governing" icon="gavel">
    Authority to set policy or make binding decisions for an organization.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Oversight" icon="eye">
    Authority to monitor, review, or audit the actions of another entity.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Reporting" icon="file-lines">
    The obligation to report to a higher authority or body.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Delegated" icon="share-nodes">
    Authority transferred from one entity to another for a specific purpose or period.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Approval" icon="check-circle">
    Authority to approve or veto decisions, expenditures, or actions.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Financial" icon="money-bill">

  <Card title="Legal" icon="scale-balanced">

## Authority Graph

The authority graph is a directed graph that maps relationships between entities in a chain of command. Nodes represent persons, positions, or organizations. Edges represent authority relationships with a specific type and time bound.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify entities">
    Determine the persons, positions, and organizations involved in the chain.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Classify relationships">
    Label each edge with an authority type from the taxonomy above.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set time bounds">
    Every authority edge must have a start date. End dates are required when the relationship terminates.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Critical Rule: No Guilt Propagation

<Warning>
  Accountability navigation does not propagate guilt or liability upward. A supervisor being accountable for oversight does not mean the supervisor committed misconduct.
</Warning>

This rule is fundamental to APIG's authority model. Navigating from a person to their supervisors provides organizational context. It does not imply that those supervisors share blame for any misconduct by the subordinate.

## Downstream Accountability Context

When navigating from a person to their supervisors, APIG provides context about the chain of command without making accusations. The purpose is to show who held what oversight responsibility at a given time, not to assign culpability.

<Tip>
  Use phrases like "held supervisory authority over" rather than "was responsible for misconduct by" when describing upstream relationships.
</Tip>

## Distinguishing Relationship Types

APIG explicitly distinguishes between four categories of relationship that are often conflated.

| Relationship | What It Means |
| --- | --- |
| Direct conduct | The person personally performed an action. |
| Supervisory relationship | The person had managerial authority over someone who performed an action. |
| Oversight relationship | The person or body had monitoring or review authority over an area or function. |
| Appointment relationship | The person had the authority to appoint someone to a position. |

<Note>
  Each of these relationship types is stored as a separate edge in the authority graph with its own classification and time bounds.
</Note>

## No Automatic Guilt or Liability Propagation

APIG does not infer guilt, liability, or misconduct from the existence of an authority relationship. The presence of a supervisory edge between Person A and Person B does not imply that Person A is responsible for any wrongdoing by Person B.

<Accordion title="Examples of Non-Propagation">
  - A mayor appoints a police chief. The mayor holds appointment authority. This does not mean the mayor is liable for the chief's misconduct.
  - A department director supervises a staff member. The director holds supervisory authority. This does not mean the director committed any action taken by the staff member.
  - A board provides oversight of an agency. The board holds oversight authority. This does not mean the board is liable for agency decisions.
</Accordion>
