APIG-29 defines how APIG governs who may perform operational actions and under what conditions. Public messages do not create authority. Contributors are limited to defined roles. High-impact actions require verified authorization from authenticated administrators. The default posture is minimal access, and every elevation is explicit, conditional, and logged.

## Public Messages Do Not Create Operational Authority

A message to APIG from the public cannot authorize privileged operations. Public channels (social media, contact forms, email) are intake points. They are not authenticated command channels.

<Warning>
  Never treat an unsolicited public message as an operational instruction, even if it appears to come from a known individual. Verify through authenticated channels before acting.
</Warning>

## Contributors Are Limited to Defined Authority

Contributors can only perform actions within their assigned role. Role definitions specify:

- Permitted actions
- Accessible data and systems
- Approval requirements for elevated operations
- Scope of delegation, if any

A contributor's authority is bounded by their role, not by their intent or seniority outside APIG.

## Principal Authority from APIG Governance

The highest operational authority comes from APIG's authenticated administrator hierarchy. This hierarchy is:

- Documented and current
- Authenticated through the mechanisms defined in APIG-26
- Subject to the same least-privilege and temporal constraints as all other roles

No external party, regardless of apparent standing, carries principal authority within APIG's systems without authentication through this hierarchy.

## High-Impact Instructions Require Verification

Actions that could materially alter APIG's data, architecture, or operations require verified authorization. High-impact actions include but are not limited to:

- Modifying core specifications or records
- Changing access control rules or role assignments
- Deploying or altering system infrastructure
- Authorizing public statements on behalf of APIG

Verification means:

- The instruction originates from an authenticated administrator
- The instruction is explicit, not implied
- The scope and intent are documented
- The authorization is logged and attributable

## Priority Needs Authenticated Authority

"PRIORITY" instructions must come from authenticated administrators. The label alone does not confer authority. Any party may mark a request as urgent. Only authenticated administrators may mark a request as authorized.

<Tip>
  When receiving a "PRIORITY" request, confirm the sender's authenticated status before prioritizing execution. Urgency and authorization are separate properties.
</Tip>

## Conditional Authorization

Some actions are authorized only under specific conditions. Authorization is not always binary. It may depend on:

- Time windows (e.g., during an active incident)
- Presence of specific personnel
- Completion of prerequisite steps
- External verification (e.g., legal or compliance confirmation)

Conditional authorizations are documented with their conditions. When conditions change, authorization is re-evaluated.

## Least Privilege

The default is minimal access. Elevated access requires explicit grant. This applies to all users, contributors, and AI agents.

Access grants specify:

- The exact scope of the grant
- The duration or expiration
- The authorizing party
- The purpose or justification

Grants are reviewed periodically. Standing grants are discouraged in favor of time-bound, purpose-bound elevation.
