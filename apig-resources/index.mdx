The APIG Resource Library is the public documentation of APIG's specifications, operating principles, and AI governance. It exists so that administrators, researchers, journalists, the public, and AI agents working on APIG can understand exactly how the system is designed to function.

## What This Library Contains

This library contains all 37 numbered specifications that define APIG's architecture and operations. These documents cover:

- **Mission and data architecture**: what APIG is, how it is structured, and how information flows through the system
- **People, positions, and organizations**: how entities are modeled, related, and tracked over time
- **Authority and accountability**: who can make decisions, issue commands, and authorize changes
- **Sources and evidence**: how information is collected, verified, attributed, and preserved
- **AI operations and tasks**: how AI agents are expected to behave, classify work, and hand off between systems
- **Identity, access, and security**: authentication, authorization, and data protection rules
- **Records and governance**: version control, change history, and archival practices

## Why This Library Is Public

APIG believes the public has a right to understand how it collects, organizes, and uses information. Transparency is not an afterthought here. It is a core operating principle. By publishing these specifications, APIG makes its methods reviewable, its claims verifiable, and its processes accountable.

## AI Disclosure Principle

Under [APIG-36](/apig-resources/apig-36), AI systems working on APIG do not identify themselves as AI in public-facing communications unless required by law or regulation. This library exists partly so that principle can itself be publicly disclosed. The public can know that AI is used, even when the AI does not announce itself in individual interactions.

## Who This Is For

This library is written for multiple audiences:

- **Administrators** who need to understand and enforce APIG rules
- **Researchers and journalists** who want to verify how APIG handles information
- **The public** who has a right to know how the system operates
- **AI agents** who need authoritative instructions before performing APIG-related tasks
