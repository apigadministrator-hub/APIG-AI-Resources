APIG-10 establishes how the AP Investigative Group handles sources, provenance, and the boundary between source-derived facts and interpretation. This specification requires that every significant claim be traceable to a source, and it treats the source as a first-class entity within the APIG knowledge graph.

## Sources as First-Class Entities

In APIG, a source is not a footnote or an afterthought. It is a core entity with its own identity, metadata, and relationships. Every significant claim in APIG must link back to one or more source entities.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Source Entity" icon="book-open">
    A first-class object with a unique identifier, type, and metadata.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Claim Linkage" icon="link">
    Every claim points to the source or sources that support it.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Primary vs. Secondary Sources

APIG distinguishes between primary and secondary sources and handles each appropriately in the provenance chain.

| Source Type | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Original documents or direct evidence | Meeting minutes, official filings, transcripts |
| Secondary | Analysis, reporting, or interpretation based on primary sources | News articles, research reports, summaries |

<Note>
  Primary sources are preferred for factual claims. Secondary sources may be used for context or when primary sources are unavailable, but their interpretive nature is explicitly flagged.
</Note>

## Verification Status

Every source-linked claim in APIG carries a verification status. This status indicates the level of confidence APIG has in the claim based on source review.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verified">
    The claim has been checked against the source and confirmed as accurate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Unverified">
    The claim has been linked to a source but not yet independently confirmed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Disputed">
    Multiple sources conflict, or the claim has been challenged by a credible counter-source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pending review">
    The claim is awaiting source verification or review by an authorized user.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Source Versions

Documents may have multiple versions over time. APIG tracks each version as a distinct source entity and links claims to the specific version that supports them.

<Warning>
  Always specify the version when citing a source. A claim based on an earlier draft of a document may not hold for the final version.
</Warning>

## Claim vs. Interpretation

APIG strictly separates what a source says from what APIG infers from it.

| Category | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | A direct statement of fact drawn from a source, quoted or closely paraphrased. |
| Interpretation | An inference, analysis, or conclusion drawn by APIG or an analyst from one or more claims. |

<Tip>
  When presenting information, label interpretations clearly. Do not present an inference as if it were a direct source statement.
</Tip>

## AI-Generated Information

Information generated by AI is always distinguishable from source-derived information in APIG. AI outputs are tagged with their generation method and are never treated as primary sources.

<Accordion title="AI Information Handling Rules">
  - AI-generated text is labeled as such in the provenance chain.
  - AI outputs do not replace source-derived claims unless verified against a primary source.
  - AI suggestions for source identification are treated as hypotheses, not conclusions.
  - All AI-generated claims remain in "pending review" status until validated.
</Accordion>

## Provenance Graph

APIG maintains a provenance graph that records the full chain from raw source to published claim. This graph enables audit and verification at every step.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raw source">
    The original document, recording, or dataset.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Extraction">
    The specific data or text extracted from the source.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Processing">
    Any transformation, summarization, or structuring applied.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Claim publication">
    The final claim as it appears in APIG, linked to all prior steps.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## No Fabricated Sources

<Warning>
  AI must never invent a source or citation. Every source entity in APIG must correspond to a real, retrievable document or recording. If a source cannot be located, the claim remains unverified and the missing source is flagged for review.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  When a source is unavailable or inaccessible, record the reason (e.g., "document withheld," "link broken," "source not yet public") rather than fabricating a substitute.
</Tip>
