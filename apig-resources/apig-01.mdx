APIG (AP Investigative Group) exists to investigate, organize, preserve, and analyze publicly relevant information about governments, agencies, organizations, people, documents, meetings, public records, news, relationships, and evidence. This page documents the core mission and operating principles that govern how APIG collects, manages, and publishes information.

## Mission

APIG's mission is to create a comprehensive, evidence-based record of publicly relevant information. This includes information about:

- Governments and their operations
- Agencies and public bodies
- Organizations and their activities
- People in public roles
- Documents and public records
- Meetings and events
- News and media reports
- Relationships between entities
- Evidence supporting claims

## Evidence-Based Standards

<Note>
  Claims require sourcing. Unknown is a valid value. Do not invent facts.
</Note>

Every piece of information in APIG must be traceable to a source. When information cannot be verified, it is explicitly marked as unknown rather than filled in with assumptions. This principle ensures the integrity of the data and maintains public trust.

## Historical Preservation

Historical information must be preserved, not deleted. When circumstances change, APIG records the change as a new event while maintaining the historical record. This creates an accurate timeline and prevents the loss of important context.

## AI as a Tool

AI assists with research, extraction, organization, and connection of information within APIG. However, substantive publication and consequential actions require human review and authorization. AI supports the work but does not replace human judgment for decisions that matter.

## AI Portability

The APIG system must not depend on any particular AI model. Persistent resources outside of conversations serve as the institutional memory. This ensures continuity and prevents vendor lock-in.

## Transparency

APIG believes the public has a right to understand how it operates. This documentation, along with published methodologies and open standards, reflects that commitment.

## Geographic Scope

APIG begins with Illinois and Wayne County. The system is designed for expansion to additional jurisdictions without major redesign, allowing growth as resources and priorities permit.
