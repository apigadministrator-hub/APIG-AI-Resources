APIG models government structure as a hierarchy from State down to individual People, with each level populated from verified sources rather than assumptions. This page describes the hierarchy model and how APIG tracks positions, vacancies, and unknowns within it.

## Hierarchy Levels

The APIG government hierarchy follows this structure:

<Steps>
  <Step title="State">
    The top-level jurisdiction, such as Illinois
  </Step>
  <Step title="County">
    County-level jurisdictions within the state, such as Wayne County
  </Step>
  <Step title="Municipality">
    Cities, towns, villages, and other municipal entities within a county
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agency">
    Departments, boards, commissions, authorities, and special districts
  </Step>
  <Step title="Position">
    Defined roles and offices within an agency
  </Step>
  <Step title="Person">
    The individual currently holding or historically associated with a position
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Position Types

APIG tracks elected, appointed, and hired positions. All three types are included in the hierarchy. Boards, commissions, authorities, and special districts are also tracked as agencies with their own positions.

## Evidence-Based Population

The hierarchy is populated from verified sources, not assumption. AI can suggest hierarchy completion, but it cannot invent positions or relationships without evidence. Every entry in the hierarchy must trace back to a source.

<Note>
  AI propagation model: AI can suggest completions for the hierarchy, but all suggestions require evidence and human review before they are committed.
</Note>

## Vacancy vs. Unknown

APIG distinguishes between two states when a position appears unoccupied:

- **Vacant**: The position is confirmed empty through a verified source, such as a resignation announcement or a vacancy declaration.
- **Unknown**: No verified data is available yet about who holds the position. This is the default state when evidence is missing.

<Warning>
  A position without a confirmed occupant is marked as unknown, not assumed vacant. Do not infer vacancy from silence.
</Warning>

This distinction prevents the spread of unverified assumptions and maintains the integrity of the data.
